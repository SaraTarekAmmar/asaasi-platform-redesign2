# Remaining Diagnostic Workbench Benchmark

## Research purpose

This continuation reviews public diagnostic patterns for **customer health**, **pricing**, and **cash runway**. The purpose is to borrow useful interaction and decision-model patterns without reproducing a competitor’s visual identity, claims, benchmarks, output language, or product architecture.

## Patterns reviewed

| Source | Public pattern observed | Useful principle | ASaaSI adaptation |
|---|---|---|---|
| [Gainsight customer health score guide](https://www.gainsight.com/blog/customer-health-scores/) | Combines product usage, support history, sentiment, and engagement; suggests explicit metric weighting and segment-aware interpretation. | A health record should show the signal basis and attach an intervention, rather than present a mysterious score. | Build a **Signal → Friction → Intervention** record. Founders state the observed signal, customer cohort, confidence, and one bounded recovery test. Avoid false precision and generic traffic-light scoring. |
| [PlanMySaaS pricing calculator](https://www.planmysaas.com/tools/saas-pricing-calculator) | Collects cost, target margin, market range, customer base, and growth; returns pricing tiers and projections. | Pricing work needs cost context, market evidence, and an explicit test design. | Build a **Value → Evidence → Test** pricing record. Ask for the buyer job, alternative, value proof, cost floor, and reversible pricing experiment. Do not display market benchmarks or generated price promises. |
| [StartupCFO runway calculator](https://www.startupcfo.ai/tools/runway) | Begins with cash balance, monthly expenses, and monthly revenue; distinguishes gross and net burn and links runway to planning windows. | Runway decisions should identify cash inputs and the next operational action. | Build a **Cash → Assumption → Decision** runway record. Founders identify cash available, monthly committed outflow, reliable inflow, and the next proof or cost decision. Label all planning values as illustrative and avoid investment advice. |

## Product decisions

ASaaSI will preserve the landing-derived editorial visual system: off-white canvas, navy type, fine grey rules, saffron only for active nodes and provenance markers, Manrope headings, DM Sans interface text, and evidence-led route objects. These workbenches will not imitate dark calculator panels, generic KPI dashboards, or competitor-specific score bands.

Each tool will expose a functional working object in the first viewport and conclude with one **bounded experiment record**, not a verdict, benchmark score, funding recommendation, or automated financial advice.

## Additional validation and positioning patterns

| Source | Public pattern observed | ASaaSI adaptation |
|---|---|---|
| [April Dunford, “A Product Positioning Exercise”](https://www.aprildunford.com/post/a-product-positioning-exercise) | Positioning begins by separating the product’s historical label from the differentiated value and by choosing a market frame that helps the buyer understand the value. | Add a **Positioning Evidence Record** that keeps the customer’s current alternative, the differentiated capability, the buyer-recognised value, and a single category/frame test together. It avoids prescribed category labels or claims of product-market fit. |
| [Koji, “Startup Idea Validation: How to Test Your Idea with Customer Interviews”](https://www.koji.so/docs/startup-idea-validation-guide) | Document assumptions, look for recurring customer-problem language, seek commitment rather than compliments, and evaluate observed behaviour rather than stated opinion. | Add a **Customer Evidence Loop** that moves from an assumption to a customer-language note to one commitment signal. Its output is a bounded next interview or test, not a validation score. |

These additions reinforce ASaaSI’s existing workbench distinction: it treats explicit assumptions, observable evidence, and a reversible next move as primary objects, rather than using AI-generated certainty or unqualified market claims.

## Activation and first-value patterns

| Source | Public pattern observed | ASaaSI adaptation |
|---|---|---|
| [Arcade, “SaaS Onboarding Best Practices”](https://www.arcade.software/post/saas-onboarding-complete-playbook) | Frames onboarding around a named activation event, then sequences setup, first value, repeat use, and expansion. It emphasises behavior-triggered movement over calendar-driven messages. | Build an **Activation Evidence Path** that names one first-value event, the setup friction preventing it, and one behavior-triggered intervention. The record ends with a cohort review date, not a benchmark target or generic feature tour. |
| [Customer Success Collective, “How to perfect customer onboarding in a startup business”](https://www.customersuccesscollective.com/onboarding-for-startups/) | Advises lean milestones, clear early success, and observation of time-to-value, activation, engagement, and drop-off. | Keep the ASaaSI workbench focused on the founder’s smallest observable path: customer intent, first value, drop-off point, and next intervention. Treat metrics as founder-provided context rather than platform-generated performance claims. |

The proposed tool continues ASaaSI’s evidence discipline: it identifies a single activation action and a small customer cohort, then asks what the team will do if the behavior changes or fails to change.
